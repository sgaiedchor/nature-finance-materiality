## Overview

Nature-related risks are becoming increasingly relevant to corporate
strategy, sustainability reporting, financial markets, and financial
institutions.

Yet a fundamental scientific question remains unresolved:

> **How does an ecological dependency or impact become measurable,
> informationally visible, and ultimately financially material?**

|---|---|---|---|

## ⚖️ Critical Perspective

The project recognises that nature-related financial materiality cannot
be reduced to a purely technical measurement problem.

Three broader tensions inform the interpretation of the empirical results:

### Market efficiency vs. persistent externalities
Financial markets may progressively incorporate nature-related
information, while ecological externalities can remain incompletely
priced.

### Monetisation vs. plural values of nature
Financial valuation can support decision-making but cannot represent
all ecological, cultural, social and intrinsic values of biodiversity.

### Optimisation vs. precaution
Where ecological thresholds, irreversibility and deep uncertainty are
present, conventional optimisation may be insufficient and precautionary
approaches become relevant.

Governance, environmental justice, stakeholder participation and the
rights of Indigenous Peoples and Local Communities are therefore treated
as important boundary conditions and research extensions of the
Nature-to-Finance Materiality framework.
