# Research Hypotheses

## Principle

The hypotheses are derived from the Nature-to-Finance Materiality Chain and
are formulated so that they can be empirically rejected.

No hypothesis assumes ex ante that biodiversity exposure is necessarily
financially material or that additional sustainability information is
necessarily useful.

---

## A1 — STRUCTURE

A1 is primarily descriptive and structural.

It is therefore organised around **RQ1 rather than directional causal
hypotheses**.

The study tests whether the observed temporal coupling between nature-related
and financial research exceeds patterns expected under appropriate null models.

---

## A2 — MEASURE

### H2a — Ecological Exposure and Financial Vulnerability

Higher underlying ecological exposure is associated with greater financial
vulnerability, conditional on conventional firm characteristics.

### H2b — ESG–Exposure Misalignment

Aggregate ESG indicators do not fully explain cross-sectional heterogeneity in
firms' underlying biodiversity and ecosystem-service exposure.

### H2c — Incremental Information

Ecological and textual measures provide incremental explanatory or predictive
information relative to a benchmark containing conventional financial variables
and aggregate ESG indicators.

---

## A3 — IDENTIFY

### H3a — Reporting Regulation

Stronger mandatory sustainability-reporting requirements increase the quality
and specificity of biodiversity-related corporate disclosure.

### H3b — Ecological Relevance

The effect of reporting requirements is stronger among firms with greater
underlying nature dependence or impact.

### H3c — Disclosure–Exposure Alignment

Reporting requirements reduce the gap between independently measured ecological
exposure and corporate biodiversity disclosure.

These hypotheses will only be interpreted causally if the empirical design
supports credible identification.

---

## A4 — TRANSMIT

### H4a — Exposure and Financing

Greater biodiversity exposure is associated with less favourable financial
conditions when that exposure is observable to financial decision-makers.

### H4b — Information Quality as a Transmission Mechanism

The relationship between ecological exposure and financial outcomes varies with
the quality of biodiversity-related information.

### H4c — Information Environment

The information-transmission effect is stronger where the initial information
environment is weaker or where corporate dependence on nature is higher.

---

## A5 — INTEGRATE

### H5a — Incremental Decision Value

An integrated model combining ecological exposure, disclosure quality,
governance and financial information provides greater out-of-sample decision
value than conventional ESG-based benchmarks.

### H5b — Complementarity

The performance gain of the integrated architecture is attributable to
complementarity across information blocks rather than to a single dominant data
source.

### H5c — Generalisation

Any incremental performance remains observable under temporal and, where data
permit, sectoral or geographic distribution shifts.

---

## Hypothesis Map

| Study | Hypotheses | Main Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | RQ/null-model tests | Structural emergence |
| **A2** | H2a–H2c | Measurement |
| **A3** | H3a–H3c | Regulation → information |
| **A4** | H4a–H4c | Information → finance |
| **A5** | H5a–H5c | Information integration |

---

## Interpretation of Null Results

Null results remain theoretically meaningful.

They may indicate that:

- ecological exposure is already captured by existing information;
- regulation changes compliance rather than information quality;
- financial actors do not price the measured exposure;
- information quality is not the relevant transmission mechanism;
- complex multi-source models do not improve on simpler benchmarks.

The empirical program therefore tests rather than presupposes the
Nature-to-Finance Materiality Chain.
