# Data and Variable Architecture

## Nature-to-Finance Materiality Project

## 1. Purpose

The empirical architecture is designed to study the transmission from ecological
exposure to informational visibility and ultimately to financial materiality.

The project therefore distinguishes three main empirical layers:

1. **Ecological Materiality**
2. **Informational Materiality**
3. **Financial Materiality**

These layers are complemented by institutional, governance and regulatory variables.

---

## 2. Ecological Materiality

Ecological materiality captures firms' dependencies on and impacts on biodiversity,
ecosystems and ecosystem services.

Potential measures include:

- biodiversity exposure;
- ecosystem-service dependencies;
- ecosystem impacts;
- sector-level nature dependence;
- location-sensitive ecological exposure;
- physical climate exposure;
- environmental pressures.

Potential sources may include:

- ENCORE;
- corporate environmental disclosures;
- biodiversity databases;
- climate datasets;
- geospatial datasets where reliable;
- sector-specific ecological indicators.

Final variables will depend on data availability, coverage and methodological validation.

---

## 3. Informational Materiality

Informational materiality captures the extent to which ecological dependencies,
impacts, risks and opportunities become observable through corporate information.

Potential variables include:

- ESG indicators;
- biodiversity disclosure;
- climate disclosure;
- sustainability-report content;
- quantitative environmental metrics;
- biodiversity targets;
- governance information;
- nature-related textual signals.

A central construct will be the:

**Biodiversity Disclosure Quality Index (BDQI)**

Potential dimensions include:

1. dependencies;
2. impacts;
3. risks;
4. opportunities;
5. location specificity;
6. value-chain coverage;
7. quantitative metrics;
8. targets;
9. governance;
10. financial consequences.

---

## 4. Financial Materiality

Financial materiality captures whether ecological exposure and nature-related
information are associated with observable financial consequences.

Potential outcomes include:

- cost of debt;
- loan spreads;
- financing conditions;
- credit risk;
- market valuation;
- risk measures;
- financial vulnerability.

One primary financial outcome will be selected before the main confirmatory analysis,
conditional on data accessibility and measurement quality.

---

## 5. Regulatory and Institutional Variables

The institutional layer may include:

- sustainability-reporting requirements;
- reporting regime exposure;
- implementation timing;
- regulatory scope;
- jurisdiction;
- sector;
- firm size;
- nature dependence.

Treatment definitions will be established only after a detailed legal and regulatory
audit.

---

## 6. Governance Variables

Potential governance variables include:

- board characteristics;
- sustainability committees;
- environmental oversight;
- executive responsibility;
- sustainability-linked governance mechanisms.

Governance variables may operate as controls, moderators or complementary information
depending on the research question.

---

## 7. Textual Information

Corporate reports may be analysed using:

- dictionary-based methods;
- supervised NLP;
- embeddings;
- large language models;
- structured information extraction.

AI-derived measures will be validated against human-coded observations where feasible.

Evaluation may include:

- precision;
- recall;
- F1 score;
- inter-coder agreement;
- sensitivity to prompt/model specification.

---

## 8. Cross-Study Reuse

The project follows a cumulative data strategy.

Measures developed in one study will, where scientifically appropriate, be reused
in subsequent studies.

**A2**
→ ecological exposure + textual measures

**A3**
→ disclosure quality + regulatory exposure

**A4**
→ ecological exposure + disclosure quality + financial outcomes

**A5**
→ integration of ecological, informational, governance and financial layers

This strategy ensures empirical continuity across the research program.

---

## 9. Data Governance

The public repository will not contain:

- proprietary databases;
- licensed ESG datasets;
- copyrighted corporate documents;
- confidential information;
- personal data;
- API credentials.

Where redistribution is prohibited, the repository will instead document:

- source;
- variable definition;
- transformation;
- access conditions;
- code required to reconstruct derived variables where legally permitted.

---

## 10. Reproducibility Principle

The objective is:

> **Reproducible research without redistributing restricted data.**

Public materials may therefore include:

- variable dictionaries;
- data schemas;
- processing code;
- synthetic examples;
- metadata;
- methodological documentation;
- replication instructions.
