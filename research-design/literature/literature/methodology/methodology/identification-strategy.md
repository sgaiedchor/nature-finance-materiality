# Identification Strategy

## 1. Identification Principle

The Nature-to-Finance Materiality project explicitly distinguishes four types of
empirical claims:

**Description → Association → Prediction → Causality**

The terminology used in each study will reflect the strength of its empirical design.

---

## 2. A1 — Structural Evidence

A1 is not a causal study.

Its purpose is to identify:

- intellectual structures;
- thematic evolution;
- temporal coupling;
- structural changes in the literature.

Evidence is based on bibliometric, network and information-theoretic methods.

Null-model and permutation procedures are used to determine whether observed
structures exceed patterns expected mechanically.

---

## 3. A2 — Measurement and Prediction

A2 primarily addresses measurement validity and incremental information.

The principal empirical question is whether ecological and textual measures provide
information beyond conventional financial and ESG variables.

The study may use:

- panel models;
- fixed effects;
- benchmark comparisons;
- incremental explanatory power;
- temporal out-of-sample validation.

Results will not be described as causal unless a separate identification strategy
supports such interpretation.

---

## 4. A3 — Causal Identification

A3 provides the main potential causal component of the research program.

The objective is to evaluate whether changes in sustainability-reporting requirements
affect biodiversity disclosure quality and ecological alignment.

Subject to legal and data validation, potential designs include:

### Difference-in-Differences

Treatment and comparison groups will be defined according to actual regulatory
exposure.

### Event Study

Dynamic specifications will examine:

- pre-treatment trends;
- anticipation;
- treatment dynamics;
- persistence.

### Triple Differences

Where credible variation exists, a third dimension such as high versus low
nature dependence may be introduced.

A triple-difference design will only be retained if its identifying assumptions
and interpretation are defensible.

---

## 5. Regulatory Audit Before Estimation

Before estimating causal effects, the project will document:

1. applicable regulation;
2. scope;
3. effective dates;
4. affected firms;
5. exemptions;
6. implementation phases;
7. relevant amendments;
8. overlapping policies.

The empirical treatment variable will follow the verified legal environment rather
than an assumed regulatory calendar.

---

## 6. A4 — Financial Transmission

A4 examines whether information quality affects the transmission of ecological
exposure into financial outcomes.

A core specification can be represented conceptually as:

**Financial Outcome =
Ecological Exposure
+ Disclosure Quality
+ Ecological Exposure × Disclosure Quality
+ Controls
+ Fixed Effects
+ Error**

The interaction term captures whether information quality modifies the financial
relevance of ecological exposure.

If credible regulatory or information shocks from A3 can be exploited, they may
strengthen identification.

Otherwise, A4 will explicitly remain an associational transmission analysis.

---

## 7. A5 — Predictive Validation

A5 is primarily predictive rather than causal.

Its central question is whether integrated nature-related information provides
incremental out-of-sample decision value.

The study will emphasise:

- temporal separation;
- benchmark models;
- out-of-sample evaluation;
- ablation;
- calibration;
- robustness to distribution shift;
- interpretability.

---

## 8. No Automatic Causal Language

The project adopts the following rule:

> **Causal claims require a causal design.**

A statistically significant coefficient alone will not be interpreted as evidence
of causality.

This principle applies across all five studies.
