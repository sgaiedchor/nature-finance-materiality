# Five-Study Research Architecture

> **Nature-to-Finance Materiality: From Ecological Exposure to Financial Decision-Making**

## 1. Why a Five-Study Architecture?

This research project is organised as a cumulative sequence of five studies.

The studies are not conceived as independent papers addressing unrelated dimensions
of sustainable finance. Each study resolves one stage of the same scientific problem:

> **How does ecological materiality become measurable, informationally visible,
> institutionally structured and ultimately financially material?**

The architecture follows the sequence:

**A1 STRUCTURE → A2 MEASURE → A3 IDENTIFY → A4 TRANSMIT → A5 INTEGRATE**

This corresponds to the broader Nature-to-Finance Materiality Chain:

> **Nature → Measurement → Information → Regulation → Finance → Decision**

Each study produces concepts, measures or empirical evidence that motivate or feed
the subsequent study.

---

# A1 — STRUCTURE

## Biodiversity, Climate and Finance Nexus:
### Bibliometric Mapping, Thematic Evolution and Information-Theoretic Evidence

### Scientific Function

**STRUCTURE THE RESEARCH FIELD**

A1 establishes the intellectual foundations of the research program.

Before analysing corporate exposure, disclosure or financial materiality, the study
asks how biodiversity has emerged as an object of financial research and whether
nature-related and financial research streams are becoming structurally integrated.

---

## Research Question

> **RQ1 — How has the intellectual structure of the biodiversity–climate–finance
> nexus evolved over time, and does the literature exhibit increasing structural
> integration between nature-related and financial research?**

---

## Data

Current corpus:

- Web of Science;
- 177 publications;
- 1998–2026;
- 103 publication sources;
- bibliographic metadata;
- author keywords;
- Keywords Plus;
- citation and co-occurrence information.

The final corpus and search date will be documented transparently.

---

## Methodology

The study combines:

- bibliometric science mapping;
- keyword co-occurrence networks;
- thematic mapping;
- thematic evolution;
- centrality and density analysis;
- Shannon entropy;
- Tsallis entropy;
- Jensen–Shannon divergence;
- Temporal Nature–Finance Coupling Index (TNFCI);
- null-model validation.

---

## Main Scientific Contribution

The contribution is not simply to provide another descriptive bibliometric review.

The study investigates whether the increasing presence of biodiversity and finance
terminology reflects:

1. growth in publication volume;
2. thematic diversification;
3. genuine structural coupling between nature-related and financial research.

The information-theoretic component and null-model validation distinguish structural
reconfiguration from mechanical growth in publication activity.

---

## Robustness

Robustness analyses include:

- alternative search specifications;
- alternative subperiod definitions;
- alternative keyword-frequency thresholds;
- network-community stability;
- alternative normalisations;
- alternative TNFCI specifications;
- permutation/null-model tests.

---

## Output of A1

A1 identifies:

- the intellectual structure of the field;
- emerging research themes;
- measurement gaps;
- information gaps;
- methodological limitations.

### Transition to A2

A1 shows how biodiversity becomes recognised as a financial research object.

But recognition in the literature does not establish that available corporate metrics
correctly measure actual ecological exposure.

This leads directly to A2:

> **If biodiversity is increasingly considered financially relevant, how well do
> existing ESG and sustainability indicators actually represent ecological exposure?**

---

# A2 — MEASURE

## Beyond ESG Scores:
### AI-Driven Climate and Biodiversity Risk in Corporate Sustainability

### Scientific Function

**MEASURE ECOLOGICAL MATERIALITY AND INFORMATIONAL MISALIGNMENT**

A2 moves from the structure of academic knowledge to firm-level measurement.

Its central concern is the potential gap between:

**actual ecological exposure**

and

**observable corporate sustainability information.**

---

## Research Question

> **RQ2 — To what extent do conventional ESG indicators capture firms'
> underlying climate- and biodiversity-related exposure, and what incremental
> information is provided by ecological and textual measures?**

---

## Theoretical Foundations

A2 primarily draws on:

- information asymmetry;
- disclosure theory;
- legitimacy theory;
- natural-capital and ecosystem-dependency perspectives.

The theoretical mechanism is that ecologically relevant information may remain
imperfectly observable through conventional ESG information systems.

---

## Core Hypotheses

### H2a

Higher underlying ecological exposure is associated with greater financial
vulnerability, conditional on conventional firm characteristics.

### H2b

Aggregate ESG indicators do not fully explain heterogeneity in underlying
biodiversity and ecosystem-service exposure.

### H2c

Ecological and textual measures provide incremental explanatory or predictive
information beyond conventional financial variables and aggregate ESG indicators.

---

## Data Architecture

The study builds on the existing corporate panel and may integrate:

- corporate financial data;
- ESG indicators;
- sustainability reports;
- climate-risk information;
- biodiversity dependencies;
- biodiversity impacts;
- ecosystem-service exposure;
- textual information;
- NLP/LLM-derived signals;
- geospatial information where data quality permits.

The existing empirical work covering international firms and the 2018–2025 period
constitutes the starting point rather than a separate research stream.

---

## Empirical Strategy

A2 is principally a **measurement and validation study**, not a causal study.

Methods may include:

- panel regressions with firm and time controls;
- fixed-effects specifications where appropriate;
- incremental explanatory-power tests;
- benchmark comparisons;
- out-of-sample predictive evaluation;
- NLP validation;
- model ablations.

---

## Benchmark Logic

The main comparison is:

**Conventional Financial Information + ESG**

versus

**Financial Information + ESG + Ecological Information + Textual Information**

The objective is to determine whether nature-specific information adds measurable
information rather than merely increasing model complexity.

---

## Robustness

Robustness analyses include:

- alternative biodiversity measures;
- alternative ESG measures;
- winsorisation choices;
- lag structures;
- sector × year controls;
- country × year controls;
- influential-observation exclusions;
- placebo outcomes where meaningful;
- linear versus machine-learning benchmarks;
- information-block ablations;
- human validation of NLP classifications;
- precision, recall and F1 measures.

---

## Output of A2

A2 produces:

1. measures of ecological exposure;
2. evidence on ESG–ecological alignment;
3. textual nature-related indicators;
4. a measurable exposure–information gap.

### Transition to A3

If ecological exposure is imperfectly represented by existing sustainability
information, the next question is institutional:

> **Can reporting regulation reduce this informational friction?**

This motivates A3.

---

# A3 — IDENTIFY

## Double Materiality in Action:
### Does European Sustainability Reporting Improve Biodiversity Disclosure?

### Scientific Function

**IDENTIFY THE EFFECT OF REPORTING REGULATION**

A3 examines whether institutional intervention improves the production of
nature-related corporate information.

---

## Research Question

> **RQ3 — Do sustainability-reporting requirements improve the quality,
> specificity and ecological alignment of corporate biodiversity disclosure?**

---

## Theoretical Foundations

A3 draws primarily on:

- institutional theory;
- disclosure theory;
- information asymmetry;
- legitimacy theory.

Double materiality is treated as a regulatory and reporting principle rather than
as an independent academic theory.

---

## Core Hypotheses

### H3a

Stronger mandatory sustainability-reporting requirements increase biodiversity
disclosure quality and specificity.

### H3b

The effect is stronger for firms with greater underlying nature dependence or impact.

### H3c

Reporting requirements reduce the gap between independently measured ecological
exposure and corporate biodiversity disclosure.

---

## Biodiversity Disclosure Quality Index

A3 will construct and validate a disclosure-quality measure covering dimensions
such as:

- dependencies;
- impacts;
- risks;
- opportunities;
- geographic specificity;
- value-chain information;
- targets;
- quantitative metrics;
- governance;
- financial consequences.

The index is intended to distinguish:

**volume of disclosure**

from

**quality of disclosure**

and from

**ecological alignment of disclosure.**

---

## Identification Strategy

The final identification strategy will follow a legal and empirical audit of the
applicable reporting regime.

Where treatment variation and identifying assumptions permit, the preferred
approaches include:

- Difference-in-Differences;
- staggered-treatment estimators;
- dynamic event studies;
- Triple Differences using nature dependence or exposure.

Causal interpretation will only be adopted when:

- treatment timing is clearly defined;
- an appropriate comparison group exists;
- pre-treatment dynamics are credible;
- concurrent regulatory changes are adequately addressed.

Otherwise, the study will explicitly report conditional associations rather than
causal effects.

---

## Robustness

The design will examine:

- pre-trends;
- anticipation effects;
- alternative treatment definitions;
- alternative event windows;
- placebo dates;
- placebo outcomes;
- staggered-treatment robust estimators;
- matching or reweighting where justified;
- concurrent policy changes;
- alternative disclosure-index weighting;
- manual coding validation.

---

## Output of A3

A3 provides evidence on whether reporting institutions reduce the
ecological-information gap identified in A2.

### Transition to A4

Improved disclosure is not an end in itself.

The economically important question becomes:

> **Does better information change the financial transmission of biodiversity
> exposure?**

This is the focus of A4.

---

# A4 — TRANSMIT

## From Biodiversity Disclosure to Financial Materiality:
### Information Quality and the Pricing of Nature-Related Risk

### Scientific Function

**TEST THE INFORMATION-TO-FINANCE TRANSMISSION MECHANISM**

A4 examines the central transition from informational materiality to financial
materiality.

---

## Research Question

> **RQ4 — Does the quality of biodiversity-related information modify the
> extent to which ecological exposure is incorporated into financial decisions?**

---

## Theoretical Foundations

A4 is primarily grounded in:

- information asymmetry;
- corporate disclosure;
- financial decision usefulness;
- nature-related risk transmission.

---

## Core Mechanism

The empirical mechanism is:

> **Ecological Exposure × Disclosure Quality → Financial Outcome**

This differs fundamentally from a simple regression of biodiversity on firm
performance.

The scientific objective is to identify whether information quality affects the
transmission of ecological exposure into financial decisions.

---

## Core Hypotheses

### H4a

Higher biodiversity exposure is associated with less favourable financing
conditions when the exposure is observable to financial decision-makers.

### H4b

The relationship between ecological exposure and financial outcomes varies with
biodiversity disclosure quality.

### H4c

The information-transmission effect is stronger where the initial information
environment is weaker or nature dependence is higher.

---

## Financial Outcome

One primary financial outcome will be selected ex ante after the data-access audit.

The preferred hierarchy is:

1. loan spreads or cost of debt where reliable lending data are available;
2. financing conditions;
3. credit-risk outcomes;
4. documented market-based outcomes.

Secondary outcomes will be treated as mechanisms or robustness analyses rather than
as interchangeable dependent variables.

---

## Empirical Strategy

Potential specifications include:

- panel fixed effects;
- exposure × disclosure-quality interactions;
- high-dimensional fixed effects;
- lagged information structures;
- regulatory or information shocks inherited from A3 where credible.

Instrumental variables will only be used if a defensible exclusion restriction can
be established.

Without credible exogenous variation, results will be presented as conditional
associations rather than causal effects.

---

## Robustness

Robustness analyses include:

- alternative exposure measures;
- alternative disclosure measures;
- alternative financial outcomes;
- lag structures;
- clustered standard errors;
- high-dimensional fixed effects;
- multicollinearity diagnostics;
- influential-observation analysis;
- high-nature-dependence sectors;
- placebo interactions;
- pre-specified heterogeneity;
- multiple-testing adjustments where relevant.

---

## Output of A4

A4 identifies whether and under which information conditions ecological exposure
becomes financially relevant.

### Transition to A5

A1–A4 generate four complementary information layers:

1. knowledge structure;
2. ecological exposure;
3. disclosure quality and regulatory effects;
4. financial transmission.

A5 asks whether these layers can be integrated into a coherent decision-support
architecture.

---

# A5 — INTEGRATE

## Nature-Related Sustainability Intelligence:
### Integrating Ecological Exposure, Disclosure Quality and Financial Materiality

### Scientific Function

**INTEGRATE AND VALIDATE THE NATURE-TO-FINANCE INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE**

A5 constitutes the integrative stage of the research program.

It does not seek to create another generic ESG score.

Instead, it tests whether combining the information generated in A2–A4 produces
incremental and generalisable decision value.

---

## Research Question

> **RQ5 — Does an integrated multi-source architecture combining ecological
> exposure, disclosure quality, governance and financial information provide
> incremental and robust decision value relative to conventional ESG-based
> approaches?**

---

## Conceptual Foundation

A5 operationalises the proposed:

**Nature-to-Finance Materiality Chain**

by integrating the three forms of materiality:

**Ecological Materiality**

+

**Informational Materiality**

+

**Financial Materiality**

---

## Core Hypotheses

### H5a

The integrated architecture provides greater out-of-sample decision value than
conventional ESG-based benchmarks.

### H5b

Any performance improvement results from complementarity across information blocks
rather than from one dominant source.

### H5c

Incremental performance remains observable under temporal and, where feasible,
sectoral or geographic distribution shifts.

---

## Information Blocks

Potential inputs include:

### Ecological Layer
- biodiversity exposure;
- ecosystem-service dependencies;
- environmental pressures;
- location-sensitive information.

### Information Layer
- ESG indicators;
- disclosure-quality measures;
- textual information;
- NLP/LLM-derived signals.

### Governance Layer
- sustainability governance;
- board or oversight variables;
- reporting structures.

### Financial Layer
- accounting variables;
- financing variables;
- risk measures;
- financial outcomes.

---

## Validation Strategy

The principal emphasis is out-of-sample validation.

The integrated architecture will be compared against:

- conventional financial models;
- ESG-only models;
- ecological-data-only models;
- text-only models;
- simpler transparent statistical benchmarks.

Evaluation includes:

- temporal train/test separation;
- rolling or expanding validation where appropriate;
- sectoral validation;
- geographic validation where feasible;
- ablation studies;
- calibration;
- stability;
- interpretability;
- missing-data sensitivity;
- data-leakage controls.

---

## Role of Artificial Intelligence

AI is treated as an **analytical instrument**, not as an assumed source of superior
performance.

Complex models must demonstrate incremental value relative to transparent benchmarks.

If AI-based models fail to outperform simpler specifications, that result remains
scientifically informative.

---

## Output of A5

A5 determines whether integrating ecological, informational and financial materiality
provides robust decision value.

It therefore closes the Nature-to-Finance Materiality Chain:

> **Ecological Exposure → Measurement → Information → Regulation →
> Financial Materiality → Decision**

---

# Cross-Study Integration

The cumulative logic can be summarised as follows:

| Study | Problem | Main Output | Feeds Into |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1 STRUCTURE** | Fragmented knowledge | Research structure and gaps | A2 |
| **A2 MEASURE** | Ecological exposure may be poorly represented | Exposure and information measures | A3 & A4 |
| **A3 IDENTIFY** | Reporting may not reflect actual exposure | Disclosure-quality and regulatory evidence | A4 |
| **A4 TRANSMIT** | Information may not enter financial decisions | Financial transmission evidence | A5 |
| **A5 INTEGRATE** | Information remains fragmented | Integrated validated architecture | Final synthesis |

---

# Shared Constructs Across Studies

To preserve cumulative coherence, the project maintains stable definitions of:

- biodiversity exposure;
- ecosystem dependency;
- ecological impact;
- ecological materiality;
- disclosure quality;
- informational materiality;
- financial materiality;
- decision usefulness.

Measures created in earlier studies will, where appropriate, be reused in later
studies rather than reconstructed independently.

---

# Identification Discipline

The research program explicitly distinguishes:

### Description
What patterns exist?

### Association
Which variables co-vary?

### Prediction
Does information improve out-of-sample performance?

### Causality
Does an identifiable intervention produce an effect?

The project will not use causal terminology when the empirical design supports only
association or prediction.

---

# Overall Scientific Contribution

The five studies collectively move the research question away from:

> **“Does biodiversity matter for finance?”**

toward the more precise and empirically testable question:

> **“When, how, through which information mechanisms, and under which institutional
> conditions does ecological materiality become financial materiality?”**

This cumulative architecture constitutes the scientific backbone of the
**Nature-to-Finance Materiality** research project.
