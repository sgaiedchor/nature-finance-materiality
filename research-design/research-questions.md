# Research Questions

> **Nature-to-Finance Materiality: From Ecological Exposure to Financial Decision-Making**

## 1. General Research Problem

The project investigates a central scientific tension:

> **Ecological materiality, informational materiality and financial materiality
> do not necessarily coincide.**

A firm may depend substantially on biodiversity and ecosystem services without
this exposure being adequately captured by conventional ESG indicators or
corporate reporting.

Similarly, nature-related information may become observable without necessarily
being incorporated into financial decisions.

The project therefore studies the mechanisms connecting three successive forms
of materiality:

**Ecological Materiality → Informational Materiality → Financial Materiality**

---

## 2. Central Research Question

> **How and under what conditions do firms' biodiversity dependencies and impacts
> become measurable, informationally visible and financially material, and what
> roles do sustainability reporting, regulation, governance and artificial
> intelligence play in reducing the frictions between these stages?**

This question deliberately avoids assuming that:

- biodiversity exposure is always financially material;
- greater disclosure necessarily improves information quality;
- regulation necessarily improves decision usefulness;
- ESG scores adequately represent ecological exposure;
- AI necessarily improves measurement or prediction.

These relationships constitute empirical questions.

---

# 3. Research Question 1 — STRUCTURE

## RQ1

> **How has the intellectual structure of the biodiversity–climate–finance nexus
> evolved over time, and does the literature exhibit increasing structural
> integration between nature-related and financial research?**

### Scientific objective

The first study establishes the intellectual foundations of the project.

It investigates whether biodiversity and finance have developed as largely
separate research domains or whether their conceptual structures have
progressively converged.

### Main dimensions

- thematic evolution;
- keyword co-occurrence;
- intellectual structure;
- temporal diversification;
- nature–finance vocabulary coupling;
- structural reconfiguration.

### Empirical strategy

The study combines:

- bibliometric science mapping;
- network analysis;
- Shannon entropy;
- Tsallis entropy;
- Jensen–Shannon divergence;
- Temporal Nature–Finance Coupling Index (TNFCI);
- null-model validation.

### Role in the research program

A1 identifies how biodiversity has emerged as a financial research object and
locates the conceptual and empirical gaps addressed by the subsequent studies.

**A1 → What does the field know, and where are its structural gaps?**

---

# 4. Research Question 2 — MEASURE

## RQ2

> **To what extent do conventional ESG indicators capture firms' underlying
> climate- and biodiversity-related exposure, and what incremental information
> is provided by ecological and textual measures?**

### Scientific objective

A2 investigates the potential gap between:

**actual ecological exposure**

and

**observable sustainability information.**

### Core issue

Conventional ESG scores may aggregate heterogeneous environmental dimensions and
may therefore imperfectly represent firms' actual dependencies on and impacts on
nature.

The study consequently compares multiple information sources rather than
assuming ESG scores constitute a direct measure of ecological materiality.

### Information blocks

Potential information sources include:

- conventional ESG indicators;
- corporate financial variables;
- ecological dependency measures;
- biodiversity exposure measures;
- climate-risk information;
- corporate sustainability reports;
- textual indicators;
- NLP/LLM-derived signals;
- geospatial information where reliable.

### Empirical logic

The study asks whether ecological and textual information provides incremental
explanatory or predictive value beyond:

**ESG + conventional financial information.**

### Role in the research program

A2 identifies whether a measurable:

> **Ecological Materiality → Informational Materiality Gap**

exists.

This measurement gap provides the motivation for A3.

---

# 5. Research Question 3 — IDENTIFY

## RQ3

> **Do sustainability-reporting requirements improve the quality, specificity
> and ecological alignment of corporate biodiversity disclosure?**

### Scientific objective

A3 investigates whether regulatory intervention reduces informational friction.

The study distinguishes three different outcomes:

**More disclosure**

≠

**Better disclosure**

≠

**Disclosure aligned with actual ecological exposure**

### Key empirical construct

A **Biodiversity Disclosure Quality Index** will assess dimensions such as:

- dependencies;
- impacts;
- risks;
- opportunities;
- geographic specificity;
- value-chain information;
- quantitative metrics;
- targets;
- governance;
- financial consequences.

A complementary measure will evaluate the alignment between independently
measured ecological exposure and corporate disclosure.

### Identification strategy

Subject to the final legal and data audit, the preferred empirical design is:

- Difference-in-Differences;
- staggered treatment designs;
- dynamic event studies;
- Triple Differences where treatment variation and identifying assumptions
  permit.

Causal language will only be used when treatment definition, comparison groups,
timing and pre-treatment trends support a credible identification strategy.

### Role in the research program

A3 asks whether institutional intervention can reduce the:

> **Ecological Exposure–Disclosure Gap**

If reporting improves, the next question becomes whether improved information
affects financial decisions.

---

# 6. Research Question 4 — TRANSMIT

## RQ4

> **Does the quality of biodiversity-related information modify the extent to
> which ecological exposure is incorporated into financial decisions?**

### Scientific objective

A4 studies the transition from informational materiality to financial
materiality.

The principal mechanism is:

> **Ecological Exposure × Information Quality → Financial Outcome**

### Potential financial outcomes

Depending on data availability and identification quality, the primary outcome
may concern:

- cost of debt;
- loan spreads;
- financing conditions;
- credit risk;
- market valuation;
- risk measures.

The principal outcome will be selected ex ante after the data-access audit.

### Scientific distinction

The objective is not merely to estimate:

**Biodiversity → Financial Performance**

but to investigate the information mechanism:

**Ecological Exposure**

↓

**Information Quality**

↓

**Financial Decision**

### Role in the research program

A4 tests the central transmission mechanism of the Nature-to-Finance Materiality
Chain.

---

# 7. Research Question 5 — INTEGRATE

## RQ5

> **Does an integrated multi-source architecture combining ecological exposure,
> disclosure quality, governance and financial information provide incremental
> and robust decision value relative to conventional ESG-based approaches?**

### Scientific objective

A5 integrates the constructs developed in A2–A4.

The purpose is not to construct another generic ESG score.

Instead, the study evaluates whether information integration improves the
identification or prediction of financially relevant nature-related risk.

### Information architecture

Potential inputs include:

- ecological exposure;
- ecosystem-service dependencies;
- biodiversity impacts;
- ESG information;
- disclosure quality;
- governance variables;
- textual information;
- financial variables.

### Validation logic

The integrated architecture will be compared against transparent benchmarks,
including:

- conventional financial variables;
- ESG-only models;
- text-only models;
- ecological-data-only models.

Evaluation will prioritise:

- temporal out-of-sample validation;
- sectoral robustness;
- geographic robustness where feasible;
- ablation studies;
- calibration;
- stability;
- interpretability;
- leakage prevention.

### Role in the research program

A5 determines whether the information produced throughout the
Nature-to-Finance Materiality Chain generates incremental decision value.

---

# 8. Integrated Research Logic

The five research questions form a cumulative sequence:

| Stage | Research Question | Scientific Function |
|---|---|---|
| **A1 — STRUCTURE** | How has the biodiversity–climate–finance field evolved? | Establish the knowledge structure |
| **A2 — MEASURE** | Do available metrics represent actual ecological exposure? | Identify measurement friction |
| **A3 — IDENTIFY** | Does reporting regulation improve biodiversity information? | Identify institutional effects |
| **A4 — TRANSMIT** | Does information quality affect financial transmission? | Test financial materiality |
| **A5 — INTEGRATE** | Does multi-source integration improve decision value? | Integrate and validate |

The resulting scientific progression is:

> **STRUCTURE → MEASURE → IDENTIFY → TRANSMIT → INTEGRATE**

or, conceptually:

> **Nature → Measurement → Information → Regulation → Finance → Decision**

---

# 9. Falsifiability

The research design explicitly accommodates null and contradictory findings.

For example:

- ESG indicators may prove strongly aligned with ecological exposure;
- mandatory reporting may increase disclosure quantity without improving
  ecological alignment;
- biodiversity exposure may not affect the selected financial outcome;
- disclosure quality may not moderate financial transmission;
- AI-based integration may fail to outperform transparent conventional models.

Such findings would not invalidate the research program.

They would identify **where the Nature-to-Finance Materiality Chain operates,
weakens or breaks**.

This falsifiability is central to the project's scientific design.
